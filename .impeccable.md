## Design Context

### Users

- A frontend engineering interviewer evaluating practical Angular, RxJS, and commerce architecture knowledge.
- A candidate presenting the project in roughly five minutes and needing every major boundary to be easy to point out.
- The primary job is to browse mocked products, understand search and route-driven data flows, and demonstrate cart state without setup friction.

### Brand Personality

- Composed, precise, quietly technical.
- The experience should communicate confidence and craft without feeling decorative, playful, or consumer-trendy.
- Copy stays concise and factual so architecture and commerce behavior remain the focus.

### Aesthetic Direction

- A light, editorial commerce catalogue inspired by premium footwear lookbooks and restrained enterprise product interfaces.
- Porcelain-tinted surfaces, ink typography, a muted citron accent, thin structural rules, and image-first product layouts.
- Product imagery is treated like a technical specimen: generous negative space, subtle catalogue numbering, and no loud effects.
- Avoid generic SaaS gradient heroes, excessive rounded cards, glass effects, dense marketplace filters, and animation-heavy presentation.

### Design Principles

1. Make the architecture legible through calm, predictable UI states rather than explanatory clutter.
2. Keep product imagery primary and purchasing controls unmistakable.
3. Use spacing, typography, and fine rules for hierarchy; reserve containers for genuinely interactive groups.
4. Give loading, error, no-results, out-of-stock, and empty-cart states a useful next action.
5. Adapt layouts for touch and narrow screens without hiding core commerce actions.
